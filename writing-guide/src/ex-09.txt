Part-2
This is a classic debate: should kids dive into a foreign language right when they start primary
school, or is it better to wait until they reach secondary school? Both approaches have their
merits, but I personally believe that starting early offers significant advantages.
The primary benefit of early language learning is that young children's brains are incredibly
receptive to new sounds and structures. They are less inhibited and more willing to experiment
with pronunciation, which is crucial for developing fluency. Think about how easily toddlers pick up
their native language – this plasticity is a window of opportunity. For example, children who start
learning a language like Spanish in primary school often develop a more natural accent and a
deeper intuitive grasp of grammar compared to those who start years later.
However, I do acknowledge that starting too early without proper resources or engaging methods
can be difficult. The key is to make early language learning fun and immersive, not a chore. This
could involve songs, games, and interactive activities.
For that reason, if primary schools can provide this kind of engaging environment, then starting
early truly sets children up for greater success in the long run, providing them with a valuable skill
for life.
