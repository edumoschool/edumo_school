Chet tilidan yozma ish topshiriqlari nomzodning til bilimini kompleks baholashga qaratilgan. Ushbu
topshiriqlar orqali quyidagi ko‘nikmalar aniqlanadi:


     • grammatik bilim
     • so‘z boyligi
     • fikrni mantiqiy va izchil ifodalash
     • uslubiy moslik
     • kommunikativ kompetensiya




1-bo‘lim: Elektron xatlar (B1–B2 daraja)

Bu bo‘limda nomzod bitta vaziyat asosida ikki xil elektron xat (email) yozadi.
1. Norasmiy elektron xat (do‘stga)


      • Hajmi: taxminan 50 so‘z
      • Uslub: oddiy, samimiy
      • Maqsad: shaxsiy fikr, hissiyot va rejalarni bildirish
      • Til darajasi: B1

Talablar: - sodda va tushunarli gaplar - kundalik leksika - fikrlar orasidagi mantiqiy bog‘lanish

2. Rasmiy elektron xat (tashkilot yoki rahbariyatga)


      • Hajmi: 120–150 so‘z
      • Uslub: rasmiy
      • Maqsad: muammo, taklif, shikoyat yoki so‘rov bildirish
      • Til darajasi: B2

Talablar: - rasmiy uslubga mos leksika - murakkab grammatik tuzilmalar - mantiqiy izchillik (coherence) -
bog‘lovchi vositalardan to‘g‘ri foydalanish (cohesion)
Muhim jihat:
Nomzod bir xil vaziyatni ikki xil uslubda ifodalay olishi kerak. Bu uning tilni real hayotiy vaziyatlarda
samarali qo‘llay olish qobiliyatini ko‘rsatadi.

Part-2:

That's a really pertinent question. Having experienced both, I can see distinct advantages and
disadvantages to online learning compared to traditional classroom settings.
On the plus side, flexibility is arguably the biggest benefit of online learning. You can often access
materials and lectures at any time that suits your schedule, which is invaluable for those juggling
work or other commitments. For instance, I can rewatch a tricky grammar explanation as many
times as needed, something impossible in a live class. Furthermore, the sheer volume of resources
available online, from interactive exercises to vast digital libraries, is often far greater than what a
single classroom can offer.
However, traditional classrooms offer a unique social dynamic that online learning struggles to
replicate. The face-to-face interaction with both the teacher and peers fosters immediate feedback
and spontaneous discussion, which can significantly boost understanding and motivation. I've
found that in-person group activities really help solidify vocabulary and speaking practice in a way
that virtual breakout rooms sometimes don't quite capture. The lack of direct, immediate human
connection can sometimes lead to feelings of isolation for online learners.

Ultimately, I believe the ideal scenario might involve a blended approach, leveraging the
convenience of online resources with the interactive benefits of in-person instruction.

Part-2
Technology is a highly debated topic, and I am of the opinion that its impact on our lives can be
seen as both positive and negative.

On one hand, technology has significantly improved our quality of life. For example, medical
advancements have led to better healthcare outcomes, with telemedicine allowing patients to
consult doctors remotely, especially during the pandemic. This accessibility is crucial for those in
remote areas who might otherwise struggle to receive medical attention.

Moreover, technology has transformed communication. Social media platforms enable us to stay
connected with friends and family across the globe, fostering relationships that might have
otherwise faded. Additionally, online learning has made education more accessible, allowing
people from various backgrounds to acquire knowledge and skills that were previously out of
reach.

However, there are downsides. The pervasive use of smartphones and social media can lead to
social isolation and mental health issues, particularly among younger generations. Furthermore,
the rapid pace of technological change can result in job displacement, as automation replaces
traditional roles.
Ultimately, while technology has undoubtedly improved many aspects of our lives, it also poses
challenges that we must navigate carefully. Balancing these benefits and drawbacks is essential for
a better future.

This is a classic debate: should kids dive into a foreign language right when they start primary
school, or is it better to wait until they reach secondary school? Both approaches have their
merits, but I personally believe that starting early offers significant advantages.

The primary benefit of early language learning is that young children's brains are incredibly
receptive to new sounds and structures. They are less inhibited and more willing to experiment
with pronunciation, which is crucial for developing fluency. Think about how easily toddlers pick up
their native language – this plasticity is a window of opportunity. For example, children who start
learning a language like Spanish in primary school often develop a more natural accent and a
deeper intuitive grasp of grammar compared to those who start years later.

However, I do acknowledge that starting too early without proper resources or engaging methods
can be difficult. The key is to make early language learning fun and immersive, not a chore. This
could involve songs, games, and interactive activities.
For that reason, if primary schools can provide this kind of engaging environment, then starting
early truly sets children up for greater success in the long run, providing them with a valuable skill
for life.

This is a really interesting question for our discussion board: should schools prioritize practical life
skills like managing money and cooking, or stick to the more traditional subjects like math and
science? It's a tough balancing act, and I think a compromise is definitely needed.

On one hand, subjects like math and science are fundamental. They build critical thinking,
problem-solving abilities, and provide the foundational knowledge for many careers.
Understanding physics or algebra isn't just about passing tests; it's about developing a logical
mindset that's useful in many aspects of life. For instance, a grasp of scientific principles helps us
understand the world around us, from weather patterns to how our bodies work.

However, we can't ignore the importance of practical life skills. Many young adults leave school
feeling unprepared for the realities of independent living. Knowing how to budget, manage
personal finances, and prepare nutritious meals are essential skills that directly impact daily well-
being and future success. I've seen friends struggle with debt because they never learned to
budget, and others who rely heavily on convenience food because cooking seemed too daunting.

Therefore, I believe schools should strive for a curriculum that integrates both. Balancing both
traditional and practical subjects will ultimately benefit students in their future endeavors.
