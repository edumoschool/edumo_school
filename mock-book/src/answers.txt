When people talk about marketing, branding often comes up as one of the most influential factors.
It’s not just about having a recognizable logo or catchy slogan—branding can actually shape how a
company performs in its industry. A strong brand helps a business stand out and, in many cases, even
take the lead in its particular market.
Think of Apple. Their products are known not only for their design and functionality but also for the
reputation they’ve built over time. Customers associate Apple with high quality, and that perception
adds value. It’s why people are willing to pay more—they’re not just buying a phone or a laptop,
they’re buying into an experience.
And that experience is often emotional. Many brands don’t rely solely on logic or technical
specifications to attract buyers. Instead, they tap into feelings. Coca-Cola is a good example. When
someone drinks it, they’re not thinking about the ingredients. What they connect with is the feeling
of happiness—a mood the brand has carefully cultivated through its advertising and global presence.
In the online world, branding takes on a slightly different form. Digital businesses use tools to
understand what their customers like. One common method is tracking browsing behavior through
cookies. These small data files help companies learn about user preferences, which allows them to
personalize recommendations and improve the overall shopping experience.
And it’s not just about making things easier for the customer. This kind of insight can lead to better
sales. When people feel that a brand understands them, they’re more likely to return, make purchases,
and even recommend it to others. That’s the power of branding—it builds trust, creates emotional
connections, and drives business forward.
So, whether it’s through storytelling, smart data use, or a reputation for excellence, branding remains
a central pillar of modern marketing.

Lecturer: During today’s seminar we will be looking at English Gothic architecture and its origins with
a specific case study of Wells Cathedral in England. The Gothic style was initially brought over to
England from France. This was at a period of time in which England was ruled from France by the
Normans, starting with William the Conqueror who first defeated the English army at the Battle of
Hastings on October 14th, 1066.
After 1072 when some smaller rebellions in northern England had been defeated, the Normans gained
complete control of the English monarchy, which they controlled until 1154. The peace that ensued in
England had a large impact on many aspects of daily life. Thousands of French words entered the
English language for the first time such as beef, fruit, city and hour. French ideas and styles, like Gothic,
also began to flow across The Channel to England too, examples of which can still be seen in the
architecture of many listed buildings. A listed building is one that is protected from alteration or
demolition because of its historical or stylistic importance. One such building is Wells Cathedral.
Construction on Wells Cathedral began in 1175 at a time when Gothic architecture as a style was in its
infancy. As a result, it is one of the first entirely Gothic buildings ever constructed. From the first
designs to the date, it was completed in 1490, Gothic architecture flourished in England. Therefore,
later additions to the building were still influenced by this Gothic style, rather than by later
architectural styles such as Tudor architecture.
Older cathedrals in England would have initially been influenced by Romanesque architecture,
alternatively known as Norman architecture in England. As the former name suggests, Romanesque
was a building style based on the skills passed on to various areas of Europe by the Romans. When the
Western Roman Empire collapsed in the 5th Century, these methods were retained by Rome’s former
colonies and developed further. One such Roman gift to the Romanesque architects was the round
arch, also known as the true arch. The Romans perfected this style by using wedge-shaped stones
called voussoirs, which created pressure that held the structure together at the top.
Cathedrals in England such as the ones in Ely and Canterbury were started before the arrival of Gothic

                                                    189
architecture. Even though parts of those two cathedrals which were constructed later are in the Gothic
style, other sections predating the arrival of Gothic architecture are Romanesque. The result is known
as eclectic because the building is constructed using more than one style.
All of these cathedrals belong to a group known as the medieval cathedrals of England. There are
twenty-six different buildings that belong to this group in total; all of which were constructed or added
to during a five-hundred-year period from 1040 to 1540.

they're long and slim rather than muscular like a lion or tiger and, unlike other members of the cat
